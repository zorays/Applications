# Bijli Haazir — Deep Team Brief
Updated: **18 November 2026** · For 20–21 November LUMS CleanTech Hackathon

## Executive thesis
The original problem (EV range anxiety) inspired a mobile energy truck. A decade of renewables practice and evolving EV infrastructure revealed two additional needs: battery-supported DC fast charging where site capacity is limited, and high-frequency swappable-battery access for compatible rider fleets. Energy sourcing and dispatch convert those services into a broader optimization opportunity, **not a guaranteed profit**.

## Where each 2026 idea came from
1. **Driver stranded away from charging → mobile truck:** Origin from original pitch; customer service at point of need.
2. **Grid feeder underpowered for a DC fast charger → large swappable BESS:** Smooth grid draw while supplying high short-duration charging power. Requires safe hardware, protection, controls and site study.
3. **Bike riders lose earning hours charging → compatible small-pack swapping:** Begin with an OEM-compatible cohort rather than claiming universal batteries.
4. **Energy cheapest at a different time or place → arbitrage:** Charge battery at a permitted, fully priced source, dispatch when demand supports ALL costs.
5. **Retail/motorway dwell time → host-location distribution:** Test location economics through written host LOIs; exploratory conversations are not signed deals.
6. **Gust assessed early funding stage → proof-focused pilot:** Show testable demand, meaningful usage, operator safety and net contribution.

## Engineering: end-to-end value chain
**Solar / approved grid procurement → metered charging hub → modular battery inventory with BMS/SOC/SOH → dispatch optimizer → (a) truck, (b) fixed site DC–DC charger + swappable BESS, (c) motorcycle swap inventory → billing, telemetry, safety and recycling.**

Engineering questions: charger power, duty cycle, pack voltage/chemistry, isolation, DC protocols, battery replacement time, hazardous transport, connector interchangeability, peak feeder kW, thermal safety, NEECA requirements, battery lifetime, software availability and audit trail.

## Illustrative arithmetic only
At hypothetical 100 kWh acquired for Rs40/kWh, 85 kWh delivered at Rs150/kWh, the energy-only spread is **Rs8,750**, *not profit*. Gross revenue 85 × 150 = 12,750; assumed input cost 100 × 40 = 4,000. Deduct cycling and aging, personnel, delivery route, charger depreciation, site margin, tax, financing and compliance. Validate actual availability of tariffs and the market charging price rather than generalizing from a directory listing.

## Go-to-market
- **Pilot geography:** one energy replenishment hub; one consenting high-use commercial host; one compatible rider fleet cluster.
- **Model:** charging fee per delivered kWh, rider subscriptions, fleet SLAs, capacity rental/backup, and prospective franchise/site revenue share.
- **Pilot scorecard:** kWh in/out, peak kW avoided, charger uptime, SOC accuracy, pack SOH, delivery turnaround, verified cash acquisition cost, degradation cost, cost per dispatch, repeat paid swap rate, host economics, margin per delivered kWh, safety events, customer satisfaction.
- **Next funding gate:** verified MVP + host/fleet LOIs + repeat paid use + compliance + credible 90-day budget.

## Pitch positioning
**20 seconds:** A network that brings stored electricity to EV demand by truck, interchangeable fixed-site BESS and compatible rider swaps.

**60 seconds:** Three pains—coverage, site grid constraints and rider downtime. Three service modes—truck, fixed-site DC–DC BESS, bike swaps. One dispatch logic—buy/store/transport where fully costed demand supports it. One pilot—energy hub, host location, rider cluster. One ask—integration, pilot partners, validation funding.

**Closing:** “The future of EV charging is not a place. It is a network.”

## Red lines
Never imply actual operational fleet, a granted patent, signed McDonald's partnership, universal battery compatibility, all-solar grid sourcing, measured environmental savings or a positive financial return without evidence. On public materials, avoid internal pricing promises and unpublished customer discussions.
